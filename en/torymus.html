Our experience

From research project to large-scale production.

The rearing of Torymus sinensis began at OASIS as a research project carried out together with IPSP-CNR (the CNR Institute for Sustainable Plant Protection).

Between 2012 and 2015 we set up 20 sites in Campania dedicated to rearing the natural antagonist of the gall-forming chestnut gall wasp. The trial produced positive results, and today OASIS is dedicated to large-scale rearing and the commercialisation of Torymus throughout Europe, with a production of more than one million insects a year.

What the service includes

Eight areas of intervention, a single integrated protocol.

The OASIS service is not limited to supplying the specimens: it covers the entire biocontrol chain, from the agronomic site survey to multi-year monitoring, through to training technical staff and the design of local multiplication facilities.

01

Establishment nuclei

Each standard batch consists of approximately 110 females and 60 males carefully selected and distributed evenly across 10 ready-to-use tubes. Selection and organic certification developed over the years.

02

Release protocol

Operational guidance on timing, quantities, release points and release methods — calibrated to the characteristics of your chestnut grove.

03

Release assistance

On request, our technicians can support the first release in the field or train your staff in the correct procedure.

04

Post-release monitoring

Verification of the establishment of the parasitoid and of the progressive reduction of the gall wasp in the following seasons.

05

Agronomic consultancy

Integrated technical support: what to do in the event of resurgences, how to combine biological control with other cultivation practices.

06

Multiplication facilities

Specialist consultancy and assistance for setting up company or district multiplication centres for Torymus sinensis, adapted to the local area.

07

Professional training courses

Theoretical and practical pathways for chestnut growers, agronomists, agricultural technicians and agricultural surveyors on the advanced agronomic management of chestnut groves.

08

Documentation for calls for tenders

Provision of technical documentation useful for reporting on RDP calls or specific measures for chestnut growing.

How we work

An intervention in four stages, tied to the seasonal cycle.

The release of Torymus sinensis follows the biological calendar: the timing is not chosen, it is respected. And above all it must be accompanied by the OASIS agronomic protocol, which is the factor that most differentiates the effectiveness of the intervention.

01
Preceding autumn / winter

Site survey and assessment

Visit to the chestnut grove, assessment of the infestation, definition of the quantity of specimens required and of the optimal release points.

02
April – May

Supply and release

The specimens are supplied at the correct time for the biological cycle. The release takes place in the field with a documented protocol.

03
Summer / Autumn

First verification

Monitoring of the establishment of the parasitoid. Verification of the presence of parasitised galls, an indicator of the success of the intervention.

04
Subsequent years

Multi-year monitoring

The stable establishment of Torymus sinensis takes 3-5 years. We remain available for periodic monitoring and corrective interventions if needed.

The agronomic protocol

Six guidelines that are not generic, but the result of 17 years of research.

The effectiveness of the release does not depend solely on the biological quality of Torymus sinensis: it depends to a decisive extent on the agronomic and cultivation context in which the parasitoid operates after adult emergence. Here are the six prescriptions we provide with every intervention.

"The prescriptions that follow are not generic recommendations: they are the direct result of more than 17 years of field experimentation, continuous monitoring and laboratory analysis carried out at more than 200 monitored release sites across Europe."

01

Suspension of drastic pruning in the two years following the releases

The dry residual galls from the previous year that remain attached to the branches are the exclusive reproductive substrate for overwintering larvae and pupae of Torymus sinensis. Extraordinary pruning or intensive rejuvenation in the two years following the releases would result in the physical elimination of part of the parasitoid population in diapause.

Excluded: drastic intervention Nov–May
02

Controlled management of the undergrowth and conservation of spontaneous flowering plants

Adult Torymus sinensis supplement their metabolism through nectar and honeydew from spontaneous flowering plants in the undergrowth. Limit mechanical shredding and indiscriminate mowing between March and May, encouraging nectar-bearing species such as Ranunculus spp., Veronica spp., Stellaria media and wild Apiaceae.

Encouraged: nectar-bearing flowering plants
03

Organic nutrition and root and foliar biostimulation

The massive formation of galls progressively weakens the host plant. The protocol calls for organic soil improvers based on mature compost or certified solid digestate (2–4 t/ha) in autumn post-harvest, and foliar biostimulants with protein hydrolysates and free amino acids (possibly with Ascophyllum nodosum) during the resumption of vegetative growth. All products must be permitted under the organic farming specifications (EU Reg. 2018/848).

Compliant with EU Reg. 2018/848
04

Ban on broad-spectrum insecticides during the parasitoid's flight window

The period 1 April – 31 May is the window of greatest sensitivity for biocontrol. Pyrethroids, organophosphates and neonicotinoids applied to the canopy would entirely nullify the investment in the releases. Where strictly necessary, only selective active substances compatible with organic production are permitted: copper, sulphur, spinosad, kaolin, white mineral oils, Bacillus thuringiensis.

Excluded: broad-spectrum insecticides 1 Apr – 31 May
05

Ban on burning pruning residues during the winter period (October–May)

The galls from the previous year present on the pruned twigs contain larvae and pupae of Torymus sinensis in diapause: burning them irreversibly destroys a substantial part of the overwintering population. The residues should be chipped with a wood chipper and left on the ground as organic mulch, or disposed of outside the release district.

Excluded: burning Oct–May
06

Integrated phenological monitoring and precise calibration of the releases

Each release is preceded by a phenological survey to verify the optimal stage of the galls (diameter >3 mm, not yet lignified) and integrated with the thermal accumulation data (degree-days calculated on a base of 10°C) provided by OASIS. This phenological synchronisation, refined over 17 years of activity, is the factor that most differentiates the effectiveness of the OASIS protocols compared with uncalibrated approaches.
