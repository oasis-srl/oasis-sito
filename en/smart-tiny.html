Research project · NRRP Mission 4

SMART-TINY: urban micro-forests, properly measured.

Development of advanced monitoring and recognition strategies for the characterisation and commercialisation of Mediterranean Tiny Forests (TFMed).

Context and objectives

Standardising urban forestation in the Mediterranean context.

The project adapts the Miyawaki method — which produces fast-growing forest micro-ecosystems — to the climatic conditions of the Mediterranean, where the practices developed in Northern Europe do not transfer automatically.

The aim is to build a knowledge platform integrating advanced biological monitoring and digital tools, in order to assess Tiny Forests across three dimensions: ecosystem services, biodiversity and economic sustainability. The expected commercial output is a "Mediterranean Tiny Forest Package" replicable by institutions and businesses.

Methodology

Four work packages, from the field to the economic model.

The project is carried out by Sara Envimob and OASIS with a multidisciplinary approach ranging from botanical survey to a twenty-year cost analysis.

WP1

Field monitoring

Biometric and floristic analysis at two pilot sites in Rome ("La Nuova Arca — Divino Amore" and "La Mistica"). The experimental design compares plots treated with organic soil improvers against unfertilised control plots.

WP2

Biodiversity analysis

Sampling of the entomofauna — pollinators and predators — and molecular analysis of the soil microbiome via DNA metabarcoding (16S for bacteria, ITS for fungi).

WP3

Modelling and digital

A web dashboard on AWS cloud to estimate ecosystem services — carbon sequestration, thermal comfort — and a digital prototype for integrated monitoring.

WP4

Economic analysis

A comparative Life Cycle Costing study between Tiny Forests and traditional urban greenery, over a twenty-year horizon.

Main results

Four findings from the 4th and 5th progress reports.

01

Fertiliser does not help

Contrary to expectations, the use of fertilisers at planting showed no significant benefit; in some cases (Cornus mas, Laurus nobilis) it reduced survival compared with unfertilised plots, suggesting nutritional imbalances or microbial alterations.

02

Structured insect communities

The TFMed host entomological communities with a high representation of native taxa and generalist species, a sign of resilient ecological networks. No invasive alien species were detected.

03

The soil reorganises

A functional reorganisation of the fungal communities is observed, towards symbiotrophic and saprotrophic components (Mortierella spp.), an indicator of an improvement in the soil's ecological state.

04

Worthwhile, but over the long term

The investment cost of a Tiny Forest is 3–4 times higher than traditional greenery, but operating costs drop sharply after the third year. The break-even point is estimated between the 15th and 20th year, without accounting for the value of the ecosystem services generated.

Conclusions

SMART-TINY confirms the ecological validity of Tiny Forests as resilient, self-sufficient tools for urban regeneration in the medium term.

The data collected, integrated into the digital platform developed by the project, form the scientific basis of the "Mediterranean Tiny Forest Package": a standardised, evidence-based framework to support institutions and businesses in adopting sustainable urban-forestry policies.
